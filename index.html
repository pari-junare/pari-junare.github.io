Hi! I am a job market candidate in Economics at the University of Bologna. In Spring 2026, I was a visiting fellow at the University of Helsinki. My research interests include time-series econometrics and empirical macroeconomics, with a primary focus on developing econometric methods that deliver credible identification and estimation of the dynamic causal effects of macroeconomic policy. My job market paper combines identification channels of external instruments and non-Gaussian shocks, yielding a consistent estimator which is robust to weak instruments, and admits valid standard inference.

Working Papers

Identification and Inference in proxy-SVARs with non-Gaussian shocks JMP

Abstract: Two frequent approaches for identifying structural VARs are external instruments, which carry economic content but are often weak, and non-Gaussianity of the shocks, which provides statistical identification but carries no economic meaning. We combine the two strategies in a single generalized method of moments framework. This hybrid approach point-identifies the target shocks while also identifying the non-target shocks up to sign and ordering. Under suitable rank conditions, the higher-order moments of the structural shocks anchor the identification uniformly over the instrument strength. Consequently, under local-to-zero proxy relevance, estimators of the dynamic causal effects remain consistent, and standard asymptotic inference remains valid. Moreover, the Anderson-Rubin confidence sets are substantially narrower than their instrument-only counterparts. The hybrid estimator is also more efficient than either source of identification used in isolation: at any fixed proxy relevance, even a weak instrument increases the efficiency of the estimator through its covariance with the non-Gaussian moment block. Under local deviations from proxy exogeneity, we provide asymptotic bias bounds and show that stronger non-Gaussianity of the shocks compresses the bias. Finally, the over-identified structure yields two mutually orthogonal specification tests for proxy exogeneity and the validity of the higher-order moment conditions. We derive their limiting distributions and provide a bootstrap procedure for finite-sample critical values. Monte Carlo simulations and two applications with identification of an oil-news shock and a Euro-area MP shock demonstrate the potential of our framework.

Two Gaussians, Too Many: A bootstrap-based approach to assess identifiability in non-Gaussian structural vector autoregressions

Abstract

Normality pre-tests on reduced-form innovations cannot detect whether two or more underlying structural shocks are Gaussian, and hence cannot detect a failure of identification in non-Gaussian SVARs. We propose a bootstrap-based approach that reveals such a failure. Under the null of valid identification and certain regularity conditions, the conditional bootstrap distribution of the maximum likelihood estimator of the impact matrix is asymptotically normal, so the diagnostic reduces to a normality test on the bootstrap replications. The impact-matrix estimator remains consistent and asymptotically normal when one shock is Gaussian and its shape parameter lies on the boundary of the parameter space, so the diagnostic is valid across the entire null hypothesis. When the number of bootstrap replications and the sample size diverge jointly at an appropriate rate, the test statistic, conditional on the data, is asymptotically pivotal. This implies that conditioning on this diagnostic does not induce pre-testing bias asymptotically. In simulations with Normal-inverse Gaussian shocks, the multivariate diagnostic is close to its nominal size under the null, its power against an additional Gaussian shock increases with the sample size, and its univariate version localizes the failure in large samples. Based on the estimates of a SVAR in the macroeconomic and financial uncertainty literature, we demonstrate its potential as a practical, robust tool for assessing non-Gaussian identification.
